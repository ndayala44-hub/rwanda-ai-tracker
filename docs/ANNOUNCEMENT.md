## Version 1 — recommended

Rwanda wrote Africa's first national AI policy in 2023 and created the continent's first AI agency in 2026.
It also ranks 111th of 121 economies on measured AI usage.

Both of those are true, and the gap between them is the most interesting thing about Rwanda's AI position
right now. So I built something to track it properly.

**Rwanda AI Tracker** is an independent, open, crowdsourced intelligence platform on Rwanda's AI ecosystem.
It takes two substantial pieces of work — the 2022 national AI readiness and maturity framework, and the
2022 economic sizing study — and turns them from static PDFs into something that can be kept current,
argued with, and built on.

**What it tracks**

→ 92 indicators across six dimensions: skills, infrastructure and compute, data strategy, public sector
adoption, private sector adoption, and ethical guidelines
→ AI readiness and maturity as separate composites, because having the preconditions and actually
deploying are different claims
→ 27 registered AI deployments, 53 organisations, and 18 policy instruments — including the ones that
do not exist yet, which is often the finding
→ Rwanda's position against comparators: the Africa AI Governance Index, Oxford Insights' Government AI
Readiness Index, and measured frontier AI usage
→ The 2022 USD 589m economic sizing, kept visually separate from current measurement so a 2022 estimate is
never mistaken for a 2026 statistic

**Who it is for**

Policymakers who need to know which specific instrument is holding a maturity level back. Investors and
development partners sizing where capability actually exists. Researchers who want the underlying
observations rather than a headline. Journalists who need a figure they can attribute. And anyone who
wants to check a claim about Rwandan AI against a named source.

**Three things I insisted on**

**A gap is never a zero.** Where something has not been measured, the platform says "data unavailable" and
lowers its coverage score. It does not quietly substitute a zero and let the average absorb it.

**Every number carries its provenance.** Click any figure and you get the definition, the formula, the
source, the collection date, the verification state and the full history. Coverage and confidence are
published beside every score, never folded into it.

**The scoring is mine, not Rwanda's.** The 2022 national framework defines indicators but no scoring
system — no weights, no aggregation rule, no maturity thresholds. Every composite on the platform is my own
computation under a documented methodology, labelled as such wherever it appears.

**Two things it is not**

This has **no affiliation with the Government of Rwanda** or any of its institutions, and it is **not an
official publication**. It is an independent, crowdsourced project. Where it cites a government source, that
source is named and linked; where it computes something, the computation is mine and is marked.

And it is **not finished**. About a third of the observations come from a named source today; the rest are
clearly labelled demo values so the platform can be evaluated end to end. You can filter to exactly which
is which. I would rather ship that honestly than publish a complete-looking dataset that is quietly
half-invented.

**This is where you come in.** Contributions from government agencies, universities, companies, investors
and researchers are what turn the labelled placeholders into measurements. Every submission will carry its
contributor's attribution and go through review before publication. If you hold data on AI deployments,
training numbers, investment, compute capacity or research output in Rwanda, I would like to hear from you.

## Notes on posting

- **Replace `[LINK]`** with your deployed URL. LinkedIn suppresses reach on posts with external links, so
  consider putting the link in the first comment and writing "link in the comments" in the post.
- **A screenshot helps.** The Overview or the Global Position view carries the argument on its own. Crop to
  a single card rather than the full page — a dense full-page screenshot is unreadable in a feed.
- **The strongest hook is the contradiction** — first in Africa on governance, 111th of 121 on usage. Both
  figures are verifiable and both are on the platform.
- **Say what you want back.** A post asking for contributions and disagreement gets substantively different
  replies from one announcing a launch.
- **Tag deliberately, not widely.** Organisations named on the platform will notice; make sure a figure
  attributed to them is one you are comfortable defending.
